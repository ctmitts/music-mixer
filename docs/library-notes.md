# Library Notes

Descriptions of what the music in this collection actually *sounds like*, for
the recommender to reason with.

**This file is yours to edit.** Mix Table loads it and hands it to the model
whenever you describe a feel, so anything you write here directly changes what
gets suggested. Genre tags in the library are inconsistent — the same lounge
aesthetic is filed under Electronica, World, Lounge, and nothing at all — so
these notes, not the tags, are what make "look-alike music with a certain
feel" work.

Format is free-form. Headings group a vibe; bullets describe artists. Add your
own families, correct anything wrong, and note pairings that worked in a real
set — the corrections are the valuable part.

---

## Lounge / nu-jazz / downtempo — the "Parisian chic" family

The Hotel Costes and Buddha-Bar aesthetic: jazz and bossa harmony over
programmed downtempo beats, ~90–115 BPM, warm and dark rather than bright.
Cocktail-hour sophistication, not big-band swing. **This is what "dancey jazz"
means here** — never Sinatra.

- **Stéphane Pompougnac** — the Hotel Costes curator and the center of gravity
  for this whole family. French filter-house and lounge; smoky, unhurried.
- **Hotel Costes** (Vol. 1–14) — the definitive Parisian lounge series.
  Downtempo, sultry vocals, jazz keys, brushed programmed drums.
- **Buddha Bar** / **David Visan** — the Paris/global sibling of Costes. More
  world instrumentation, oud and tabla against downtempo beats.
- **Tosca** — Viennese dub-jazz duo, hypnotic and slow. Superb bed for long
  blends.
- **Röyksopp** — Norwegian electronic, melodic and melancholy. The bridge from
  this family up into proper dance.
- **Thievery Corporation**, **Zero 7**, **Bonobo**, **Jazzanova**,
  **De-Phazz**, **Koop** — the wider nu-jazz family if you add more.
- **Grace Jones** — *Libertango* especially: tango-disco, imperious, and a
  perfect Costes-adjacent centerpiece.
- **Nina Simone** — sits at the seam between this family and vocal jazz. The
  remixes (Groovefinder etc.) mix like lounge; the originals are fluid tempo.

**Mixing:** long blends, key-matched. Most of it is steady enough to beatmatch
but sounds better with a slow crossfade than a hard cut.

## Reggae / dub / ska / dancehall

A deep section — roughly 350 tracks, and one of the collection's real
strengths.

- **Bob Marley (& The Wailers)** — roots foundation. Steady ~75–90 BPM.
- **Steel Pulse** — Birmingham roots reggae, political, tight horn sections.
- **Sublime** — ska-punk/reggae fusion, faster and rowdier; bridges into rock.
- **Pepper**, **Slightly Stoopid**, **The Expendables** — modern California
  reggae-rock. Same lane as Sublime, good for a sunny mid-set stretch.
- **Dub Congress** — dub proper: spacious, delay-drenched, ideal under other
  material because the mids are largely empty.
- **Sean Paul** — dancehall, ~100 BPM, high energy. Bridges reggae into
  hip-hop and pop.
- **Rancid** — punk with heavy ska DNA; the aggressive edge of this family.

**Mixing:** reggae's half-time feel means ~90 BPM tracks layer naturally under
~180 BPM material, and against 120 BPM house via double-time. Dub tracks are
the best bridges in the entire library.

## Hip-hop — mostly the underground/backpack end

- **Atmosphere**, **Aesop Rock**, **Slum Village**, **People Under The
  Stairs**, **Josh Martinez** — jazzy, sample-based, warm. Pairs beautifully
  with the lounge family (shared jazz sampling roots).
- **DJ Shadow** — instrumental, cinematic, sample collage. *Midnight in a
  Perfect World* is a classic bridge track.
- **The Avalanches** — plunderphonic and euphoric; sits between hip-hop and
  dance.
- **Dr. Dre**, **Jay Z**, **50 Cent**, **Chamillionaire**, **Mac Dre** — the
  commercial/West-coast end. Harder, more club-ready.
- **Wyclef Jean** — hip-hop with heavy reggae and world influence; a natural
  bridge to the reggae section.

**Mixing:** ~85–100 BPM. Double-time relationships to house make these useful
transitions in and out of dance material.

## Vocal jazz & standards — the big-band end

Large (Sinatra alone is 400+ tracks) and almost entirely **fluid tempo** —
live rhythm sections breathe, so the beat grid is unreliable. Treat as texture
and interlude, never as beatmatch partners.

- **Frank Sinatra**, **Dean Martin**, **The Rat Pack** — swing and ballads.
- **Ella Fitzgerald** — the finest vocal jazz here; wide dynamic range.
- **Cole Porter**, **George Gershwin** — composer collections, standards.

**Mixing:** key-compatible long fades only. Superb for opening a set, closing
one, or a deliberate palate-cleanse — but they will not beatmatch, and Mix
Table marks them FLUID for that reason.

## Classic rock & psychedelia

- **The Beatles** (258 tracks) — everything from skiffle to studio psychedelia.
- **Pink Floyd** — long-form, spacious, dynamic. Great for extended blends.
- **The Rolling Stones**, **The Doors**, **Jimi Hendrix**, **Steppenwolf**,
  **Fleetwood Mac**, **Santana** — 60s/70s canon. Live drumming means slight
  tempo drift; check the FLUID flag.
- **Bob Dylan** — mostly acoustic and fluid; a texture source, not a groove.

## Indie / alternative

- **Death Cab For Cutie**, **Modest Mouse**, **Spoon**, **The Strokes** —
  guitar-driven, mid-tempo, melancholy-to-wry.
- **Passion Pit** — synth-pop, bright and danceable; the most mixable of this
  group.
- **Nirvana**, **Red Hot Chili Peppers**, **Marilyn Manson** — the heavy end.

## Electronic / dance

- **Daft Punk** — French house; *Robot Rock* and the filter-house material sit
  right next to the Costes family stylistically.
- **Darude**, trance and big-room material — the peak-time end.
- **Röyksopp**, **The Avalanches** — melodic, downtempo-adjacent.

**Mixing:** 120–136 BPM, rock-solid grids. This is where beat sync earns its
keep.

## Classical

- **Yo-Yo Ma & Ton Koopman** (Bach), **Brahms**, **Mozart's Requiem**,
  **Vivaldi** — all fluid tempo by nature.

**Mixing:** key badge and long automatable fades only. A Bach cello line under
a dub track is a legitimate move; beat-syncing a Requiem is not.

## Not music — exclude from suggestions

- **Eckhart Tolle** (~116 tracks) — spoken-word lectures.
- **Dane Cook** (~29 tracks) — stand-up comedy.
- Voice memos with date-stamp filenames (`20110203 132835`) — phone recordings
  that ended up in the library.

These are tagged like music and will otherwise surface as "compatible" because
speech has a key and a tempo. Never suggest them.

## Bridges that work in this collection

- **Dub → house**: dub's half-time ~85 BPM double-times cleanly to ~170, and
  its empty mids leave room for an incoming track.
- **Lounge → hip-hop**: shared jazz sampling vocabulary; Costes into Atmosphere
  or DJ Shadow is nearly seamless.
- **Classical → downtempo**: key-match a cello or piano line over a Tosca or
  Bonobo bed, 30-second fade.
- **Reggae → dancehall → hip-hop**: a natural energy ramp, ~75 → 100 BPM.
- **Vocal jazz as a reset**: drop a Sinatra ballad to clear the floor's palate,
  then rebuild from lounge.
